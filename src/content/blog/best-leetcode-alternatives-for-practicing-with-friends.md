Search "LeetCode alternatives" and you'll get a dozen lists comparing problem counts and language support. Almost none of them ask the question that actually matters if you get bored grinding alone: which of these lets you practice _with_ someone instead of just next to them.

Here's an honest rundown, from that angle specifically.

## Codewars

Codewars turns problems into "kata" ranked by difficulty, with a strong community focus on comparing solutions after you submit. That peer-review loop is genuinely valuable for learning idiomatic code. What it doesn't have is any live, head-to-head element. You solve, then you compare notes. There's no clock counting down while someone else races you to the same answer.

## HackerRank

HackerRank's strength is breadth: interview kits, certifications, company-specific tracks. It's built for structured, solo prep, and it's good at that job. Like LeetCode, the loop is still read-solve-move-on. If you want to feel the pressure of a live match instead of an untimed exercise, HackerRank isn't built for that.

## CodinGame

CodinGame gets the closest to "fun" of the traditional platforms, wrapping problems in game-like puzzles and clash-of-code events. It's the most game-like of the established options, and worth trying if you like a more visual, puzzle-driven style. The clash mode is timed and multiplayer, which is the right idea, but it's a side feature bolted onto a much bigger solo catalog rather than the whole point.

## StackDuel

This is the one we're obviously biased about, so take it as such: StackDuel is built entirely around the head-to-head loop. You and another player (or a full lobby) get the same problem, judged the instant either of you submits. No comparing notes afterward, no separate "multiplayer mode" tucked away from the main catalog. It's smaller than the platforms above right now. We're in alpha, we support three languages, and we're not trying to out-catalog LeetCode's company-tagged question bank. If what you actually want is to practice against a real person instead of a static list, that's the entire product.

## The honest recommendation

If you need the widest problem catalog and company-specific interview tags, LeetCode and HackerRank still win, and we're not going to pretend otherwise. If you've already got the fundamentals and want practice that actually feels like pressure, pull a friend into a duel and see how differently you solve the same problem you'd have breezed through alone.
